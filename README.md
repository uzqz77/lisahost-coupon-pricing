## How to apply the code without losing the discount

The flow is short and there is exactly one place to get it wrong.

1. Open the plan page and pick your configuration and billing cycle. The link you use matters here, because the plan IDs are product-specific.
2. On the order confirmation page, find the coupon field and enter `TS-CBP205DQJE`.
3. **Click Validate.** The order summary should now show a reduced total. If the total has not changed, the code did not apply.
4. Pay only after you see the discounted number.

The failure mode is not subtle but it is common: people paste the code, glance at the total, and assume the system handled it. It does not. The button is the step.

## What the extra spend actually buys

The catalog has a lot of near-identical 1-core, 1GB annual plans with wildly different prices. The differences are IP type and route, and it is worth pricing those out explicitly.

**¥199 vs ¥499 in the US: ¥300 a year for the residential attribute.** Both are 1 core, 1GB RAM, 10GB disk. The ¥199 plan gives you 50 Mbps and 200GB monthly on a non-native IP. The ¥499 plan gives you 50 Mbps, 600GB monthly, and a dual-ISP residential IP. You are paying ¥300 a year, or about ¥25 a month, for an address that looks like a home connection to platform risk engines, plus triple the traffic. If you are running ad accounts or TikTok profiles, that is the cheapest line item in your whole operation. If you are running a personal proxy or a test box, it is ¥300 wasted.

**¥399 vs ¥499 in the US: the cheaper plan has the faster port.** The 4837, New York and Chicago annual plans all cost ¥399 with 100 Mbps and 600GB. The 9929 plan costs ¥499 with 50 Mbps and 600GB. You are not paying more for more bandwidth; you are paying more for the 9929 return route, which is the one people cite when they talk about evening peak stability from mainland China. Choose 4837 for a US-east presence or a bigger pipe, choose 9929 for the route.

**Monthly vs annual on the same 9929 residential spec: ¥88/mo against roughly ¥41/mo.** The monthly Basic tier (1 core, 1GB, 20GB, 60 Mbps, 2TB) runs ¥88 a month, or ¥1,056 a year. The annual plan (1 core, 1GB, 10GB, 50 Mbps, 600GB) runs ¥499, about ¥41 a month. You give up 10GB of disk, 10 Mbps of port speed and 1.4TB of monthly traffic, and you save more than half. For a single TikTok account or a small shop, that trade is not close.

**Hong Kong ¥566 vs ¥699: ¥133 for residential.** CMI native at ¥566 gives you a 30–50ms class route and a native IP. iCable at ¥699 gives you a residential IP from a Hong Kong cable operator plus 100 Mbps and 1000GB instead of 50 Mbps and 600GB. The ¥133 difference buys both the IP type and double the port. If you are doing anything Hong Kong-adjacent that cares about IP reputation, that is the better ¥133 you will spend in this catalog.

**Japan ¥499 vs ¥899: ¥400 for a home line.** The native IP annual plan is ¥499 with 100 Mbps and 600GB. The ISP residential VDS is ¥899 with 100 Mbps and 1000GB. The premium is entirely about the address belonging to a Japanese residential ISP. Buy it if a Japanese platform is checking; skip it if you just want a Tokyo box with a clean native IP.

## Where LisaHost is the wrong buy

The interface is entirely in Chinese, prices are in RMB, and payment runs through Alipay and WeChat Pay first. If you do not read Chinese and do not have either wallet, the buying experience alone will be friction.

Taiwan does not support TikTok. This is stated by the vendor, not inferred, and it eliminates the entire Taiwan line for the most common reason people come to this brand.

Singapore and the UK run BGP international routing, not mainland-optimised routing. LisaHost itself suggests relaying through Hong Kong or Japan for Singapore, quoting roughly 30ms from Hong Kong and 70ms from Japan as the relay legs. If you are in mainland China and want a direct connection, these two are not it.

It is not a budget host. A pure datacenter-IP VPS from a cut-rate provider costs less than ¥199 a year. What you are buying here is IP type and route quality, and you pay for both. If your only criterion is price per gigabyte of RAM, this catalog will look expensive.

The ¥2/day trial covers the US CN2 GIA line only. It is not a site-wide try-before-you-buy.

One honest data point on reliability: third-party write-ups report a UK node outage caused by upstream carrier Vorboss reclaiming IP ranges, which LisaHost handled by switching to a backup arrangement and compensating affected users with multiple times their remaining service length. That is a good response to a bad situation, and it is also a reminder that residential IP inventory depends on carriers who can change their minds.

For context, the company has operated since 2017 out of Hong Kong, runs KVM virtualisation with NVMe storage across the line, and offers Chinese-language support tickets with responses typically within hours and complex issues taking about a day. Third-party latency measurements put US 9929 at roughly 187ms nationwide and about 154ms from Shanghai and Hangzhou, Hong Kong CMI at 30–50ms, Taiwan at 60–80ms and Japan at 70–100ms, with evening peak downloads at 80–90% of the advertised port speed. Those are tester numbers, not vendor claims, and your route will vary.

## FAQ

**Does the coupon stack with annual pricing?**
Yes, in the sense that it applies to the annual price shown in the plan list. A ¥199 annual plan becomes about ¥179. Some roundups claim annual billing is separately discounted 20% and the code stacks on top of that to reach roughly 72% of list; the arithmetic on the entry plan does not support it, so plan for 10%.

**Does it still work on renewals?**
One source describes the code as a permanent 10% that applies to every renewal, not just the first invoice. That is a third-party claim with no vendor page behind it, and there is no published renewal price list. Assume the first invoice is discounted and verify at renewal time.

**Can I use one code on multiple orders?**
Nothing in the sources limits it to a single order, and it is described as a site-wide code rather than a one-time new-customer voucher. Use it on each order and click Validate each time.

**Which plan is the “¥179 per year” one?**
The US 9929 non-native IP plan, 1 core, 1GB RAM, 10GB SSD, 50 Mbps, 200GB monthly, listed at ¥199/yr and about ¥179 after the code. It is the cheapest thing in the catalog, not a residential plan.

**Can I run TikTok on the Taiwan node?**
No. LisaHost states that the Taiwan node does not support TikTok. For TikTok, use the US, UK, Korea or Vietnam residential lines.

**What happens if I forget to click Validate?**
The order goes through at full price. There is no automatic application. If you catch it immediately, the 48-hour refund window is the fallback.

**Is the ¥2 trial available on every plan?**
No, it covers the US CN2 GIA line only.
